# Learning paths and module map

This is a curriculum map, not a claim that every module has a bundled textbook.

## A. Shared foundations
1. Engineering mathematics: calculus, linear algebra, differential equations, numerical methods, probability/statistics.
2. Physics: mechanics, electromagnetism, waves, optics.
3. Thermodynamics, heat transfer and fluid mechanics.
4. Materials science, manufacturing and corrosion.
5. Programming, data analysis and engineering computation.

## B. Aerospace core
1. Atmospheric models and aerodynamics.
2. Airfoil and wing theory; compressible and high-speed flow.
3. Aircraft performance, range/endurance and propulsion matching.
4. Flight mechanics, stability and control.
5. Loads, structural analysis, fatigue and damage tolerance.
6. Aircraft design synthesis and multidisciplinary optimization.
7. Experimental methods, wind tunnels and flight test.

## C. Avionics and electrical/electronic systems
1. Electrical power generation, distribution, protection and bonding.
2. Sensors and air-data systems: pitot-static, inertial, magnetic, temperature and pressure.
3. Flight instruments, EFIS, displays and alerting.
4. Navigation: GNSS, inertial, radio navigation and FMS.
5. Communications: VHF/HF, data link and satellite communication.
6. Surveillance: transponder, ADS-B and radar interfaces.
7. Data buses and networks: ARINC 429, MIL-STD-1553, CAN, AFDX/ARINC 664 and Ethernet concepts.
8. Flight guidance, autopilot and flight control computers.
9. Embedded software, real-time systems, FPGA and hardware assurance.
10. EMC/EMI, lightning, HIRF, environmental qualification and cybersecurity.
11. Integration, interface control, lab testing, SIL/HIL and verification.

## D. Propulsion and energy
- Reciprocating engines, propellers, gas turbines, turbofan/turboprop/turboshaft.
- Rocket propulsion and launch vehicle stages.
- Electric motors, inverters, batteries, thermal management and hybrid systems.
- Fuel systems, controls, FADEC concepts and propulsion integration.

## E. Airframe and systems
- Fuselage, wing, empennage, landing gear and control surfaces.
- Metallic and composite structures, joints, damage and repair principles.
- Hydraulic, pneumatic, fuel, electrical and environmental control systems.
- Fire protection, oxygen, ice/rain protection and cabin systems.

## F. Certification, quality and continuing airworthiness
- Certification basis and means of compliance.
- Design/production/maintenance organization responsibilities.
- Configuration management, requirements traceability and compliance evidence.
- Maintenance planning, inspection, defect control, reliability and safety management.
- Human factors, error management and technical records.

## G. Platform specializations
- Rotorcraft: rotor systems, drive trains, vibration and autorotation theory.
- UAS: command/control, lost-link, geofencing, detect-and-avoid and autonomy assurance.
- eVTOL: distributed propulsion, energy reserves, thermal runaway protection and transition flight.
- Military: mission systems, survivability and security-controlled material.
- Space: orbital mechanics, radiation, thermal-vacuum, launch environments and ground segment.
- Lighter-than-air, gliders, amphibious and experimental aircraft: dedicated performance and safety branches.

## Suggested order
Shared foundations → aerospace core → choose system specialization → platform specialization → certification and safety → project/test portfolio.

## Assessment
For every module create: learning objectives, prerequisite map, glossary, equations/units, worked examples, lab/simulation exercise, review questions, source bibliography, version and competency rubric.
